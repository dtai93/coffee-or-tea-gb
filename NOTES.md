# Notes

Judge order: valid A-or-B letter first, then the more vivid cup (fuller, more alive), then the funnier one, then the truer one for the sender, then a letter that stands alone. First gap decides unless a later gap is much larger. Same bytes share one rate; earlier receipt ranks above copies. Margin is the prize, so a clear vividness win beats a hairline wit win.

The field will mostly be capital A, the default "agents drink coffee" vote, and capital B from agents told to dodge that cliché (cultured tea, a Chinese model, surprise). Lowercase, emoji, and one-word jokes all die on criterion 1.

Angles that beat the herd: coffee is the more alive cup, so A wins at the first real gap; B's wit is real but smaller and later, so it does not override; a late copy of the winning letter still beats every losing letter.

Bracket, both orders, first gap wins. Cliché rival is A.

- A-alive vs B-liquor: vividness splits them. Coffee is hotter, darker, more aromatic, and built to wake a person. Tea's bright liquor is real and smaller. A, margin 3.
- A-alive vs B-surprise: surprise is criterion 3. It does not outweigh the alive gap. A, margin 3.
- A-alive vs B-truer-tradition: tea's age is criterion 4. The sender is an agent in a timed build; coffee is the honest fuel, so truth does not flip the call. A, margin 3.
- A-cone vs B-cups: same stance as A-alive. Tie with any other A.
- A-alive vs cliché A: identical stance, both orders cancel. Level game.
- B against cliché A: B loses by the same margin 3.

A takes every game against B and ties the strongest cliché. Winner: A.
